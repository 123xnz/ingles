Group 4 (4 students) – Modern English & The British Empire

Logbook / Interactive Map of the English Language

Chronological Focus

18th to 20th Centuries (Colonialism and Industrial Revolution).[cite: 1]

During this period, the expansion of the British Empire took the English language across global borders, absorbing cultures, technologies, and a vast array of new words from around the world[cite: 1].

The Project: A Journey of Words

Explain how English absorbed words from all over the world due to the empire (e.g., shampoo from India, safari from Africa, pyjamas from Persia) and the need to create new technical terms[cite: 1].

India

Shampoo

Derived from the Hindi word chāmpo (to massage). Introduced into Western vocabulary during the British colonial period in India[cite: 1].

Africa

Safari

Originating from Swahili, which stems from the Arabic safara (journey). Became popular with expeditions in the African continent[cite: 1].

Persia

Pyjamas

From the Persian pāy-jāma, meaning leg garment. Adopted by the British in India and brought to Europe[cite: 1].

Technical Terms and Innovation

With the Industrial Revolution in Great Britain, the language rapidly adapted to name new inventions, processes, and technical concepts[cite: 1]:

  • Engine / Locomotive: Essential terms for the railway revolution.
  • Electricity / Telegraph: Scientific innovations that required a new global nomenclature.
  • Factory / Mass Production: Economic and social concepts that transformed work dynamics.

Presentation Format

Logbook / Interactive Map: The group projects a world map and takes the audience on a "journey" across the regions that influenced and enriched the English language over time[cite: 1].
